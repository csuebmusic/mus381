## curatorial direction

The bank has enough variety for distinct pieces without overwhelming students. The aesthetic frame is musique concrète–style found sounds: recognizable everyday material that students can recontextualize, manipulate, and arrange.

A second frame is the envelope. Students hear each sound's attack, sustain, and release. The bank includes sounds across the envelope categories below.

### envelope variety

The categories are roughly balanced, and each has sounds students can find:

- sharp attack, no sustain, fast release: clicks, snaps, claps, drops, hits, taps, quick percussive sounds
- sharp attack, short sustain, fast release: single plucked or struck notes, short voiced syllables, single bird calls
- soft attack, long sustain, slow release: bowed strings, sustained voice, drones, hums
- continuous sustain: running water, rain, traffic, wind, machinery, refrigerator hum
- complex envelopes: a door closing (attack, decay, attack), a spoken sentence (multiple onsets), a glass filling (rising sustain)

Students don't see these categories as a checklist. When the Mon Wk 3 lecture introduces the envelope, students find sounds in the bank for each shape.

### scope

- 40 to 80 sounds in total
- a mix of short sources (under 5 seconds) and longer ones (5 to 30 seconds)
- mostly 48 kHz, 24-bit WAV, the Project 1 export format
- mostly mono or true stereo, without unusual stereo processing

---

## what to leave out

- copyrighted music or recognizable musical phrases
- loops or tempo-locked material
- anything offensive, alarming, or distressing, unless it has a clear teaching purpose
- voice recordings of identifiable people without their consent
- anything long enough to become the whole piece when imported

## folder structure

```
/public/sample-banks/project-01/
  attack-sharp/
  attack-soft/
  sustain-long/
  sustain-short/
  texture-continuous/
  voice-and-language/
  found-objects/
  natural-environment/
  mechanical-electronic/
  README.txt   <- what's in the bank, license terms
```

The categories are a starting point; adjust them to what the bank holds. Students browse by character (envelope) or by source type.

The `README.txt` in the bank, written for students, covers:

- that every sound is free to use for Project 1
- provenance (Inés's recordings, public-domain archives, Freesound CC0 material)
- how to credit, where crediting applies
- the project rule: only sounds from this folder
