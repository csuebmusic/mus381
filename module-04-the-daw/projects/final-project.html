Module 04 · The DAW

final project

A finished piece, 2 to 3 minutes long, built in Ableton from sound you recorded yourself.

This is the last thing you make in the course, and it should show what you can now do: record sound, edit it, sample it, arrange it, and mix it into a finished piece. Build it in Ableton Live. The approach is open. You can arrange edited audio clips the way you did in Project 1, build the piece from samples you trigger and chop with Simpler and the Drum Rack, or combine both. There's no required style. What the piece has to do is sound like you meant every choice in it.

constraints

  • The piece is 2 to 3 minutes long.
  • Every sound in the piece must be one you recorded yourself. Your Module 3 sample library counts, and so does anything new you record between now and the deadline. Don't use pre-recorded, found, or downloaded sound or pre-made loops. (One exception, with its own rule: see the sourcing section below.)
  • Work in Ableton Live.
  • Export the final piece as a WAV, 48 kHz, 32-bit (the Module 4 standard).
  • Name the file lastname-final.wav (see Submission below for where each file goes).

what "demonstrating fluency" means

A successful piece shows five things.

  • You recorded and chose your material with intention.
  • You edited deliberately, and every cut, fade, and placement is a decision you made.
  • You can load a sound into Simpler or the Drum Rack, chop it, and play it. Sample where it serves the piece; it's optional.
  • You arranged the piece across the Arrangement timeline with a planned shape.
  • You mixed it: levels are balanced, each element has space, and nothing clips.

You don't need to use every skill equally. Three skills used with control can make a complete piece.

form, again

You met form in Project 1: the overall shape of a piece across its full length, the thing that turns a sequence of sounds into a piece of music. The same applies here, with more room. Two to three minutes needs a deliberate shape, whether that's sections that contrast and return, a single arc from sparse to dense, or a steady groove that holds a listener's interest for its full length. Before you call it done, you should be able to sketch your piece on paper. For the longer treatment, see the form section of Project 1.

recording and sourcing your sound

The rule is simple: you recorded everything you hear. The artists in this module's listening built music from records they dug up; you build it from sound you record yourself. Your Module 3 library is the obvious starting point. Beyond it, record more: the studio, your phone at 48 kHz, an instrument, a hallway, water, a voice.

You may use a sound from another student's library if you ask them first, they agree, and you credit them. Keep a short credits file, lastname-final-credits.txt, in your working folder, listing any borrowed sound and the person it came from. If everything is your own, the file can simply say so.

process and schedule

The project has two drafts before the final version:

  • Draft 1 is due Wed Wk 14: a complete rough pass. The whole piece exists end to end, even if it's unmixed. You submit it to your working folder, and your instructor gives you written feedback to revise on.
  • Draft 2 is due Wed Wk 15: the revision, where you've taken the Draft 1 feedback and acted on it.
  • The final version is due during finals week, polished, mixed, exported, and submitted (see Submission below).

A few working habits:

  • Start in Wk 12, not in Wk 14.
  • Save versions as you go: lastname-final-v1, v2, v3.
  • Use lab time to get feedback from your instructor and your classmates while you build.

rubric

The final project is graded out of 100 points across four dimensions.

technique & tools (35)

  • The semester's skills are audible and used well: editing, sampling, arrangement, mixing
  • Where you sample (Simpler, Drum Rack), it's done with control, not by accident
  • The DAW is used deliberately; the piece shows real work, not an afternoon's throw-together

form & shape (30)

  • The piece has a deliberate shape across its 2 to 3 minutes: beginning, middle, end, in some form
  • Pacing is intentional; the length fits the material, without padding or rushing
  • The piece changes over time instead of holding one texture for three minutes

sound material & sourcing (20)

  • Every sound is your own recording, or borrowed with permission and properly credited
  • Material is chosen and recorded with intention, and prepared cleanly
  • A credits file is present, even if it just says everything is yours

mix & craft (15)

  • Levels are balanced: every element is audible at the level you intended
  • Nothing clips; the export is clean
  • Elements have space; the stereo field is considered

what isn't graded

  • Style or genre isn't graded: a beat-driven track and an abstract sound-collage can both score 100.
  • Technical perfection isn't expected. The rubric looks for intention and craft.
  • Where your length falls within the 2-to-3-minute range doesn't affect the grade. A 2:10 piece and a 2:55 piece are both fine; don't pad to hit a number.
  • Your instructor grades by the rubric, and personal taste isn't part of it.
