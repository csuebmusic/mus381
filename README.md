# MUS 381 · Essentials of Music Technology

Course materials, teaching tools, and TA notes for MUS 381 at CSU East Bay: readings, handouts, interactive tools, listening assignments, projects, exams, and session-by-session teaching notes, organized by module. The student-facing pages are served through GitHub Pages at [`csuebmusic.github.io/mus381`](https://csuebmusic.github.io/mus381/).

Students start with the [syllabus](https://csuebmusic.github.io/mus381/syllabus.html) for the schedule, grading, policies, and what to bring. This README is the teaching-side map.

## the course

MUS 381 is a 3-unit introduction to music technology for students with little or no prior experience: digital audio, editing, mixing, recording, sampling, and DAW work, taught hands-on and project by project. Students listen, analyze, and make their own work in response throughout.

The semester runs in four modules. Modules 2, 3, and 4 each end in a deliverable. The class meets Mondays and Wednesdays, 3:00 to 4:40 PM, in MB2525. Mondays are lecture (a concept and a listening example), and Wednesdays are lab (a guided hands-on session at the stations). Wk 1 and Wk 4 meet Wednesday only. Wk 13 meets Monday only. The dated calendar, grading weights, and course policies are in the [syllabus](https://csuebmusic.github.io/mus381/syllabus.html).

## modules

Each module folder has a `README.md` with the module's purpose, learning outcomes, live page links, and session-by-session teaching notes, plus `lessons/` (readings, tools, and lab handouts in encounter order). Modules 2, 3, and 4 also have `listening/` and `projects/`. The live student pages for each module are linked below, with the label each page shows in its header.

### Module 2: digital audio, editing & mixing · Wks 2–5

Digital audio fundamentals, then editing, mixing, and dynamics (compression and limiting) in Audacity, an audio editor where cuts and effects change the audio data. The listening is musique concrète. The deliverable is Project 1, a musique concrète piece of 90 seconds to 2 minutes built from the project sample bank on the class server and handed in as `lastname-project01.wav` (48 kHz, 24-bit), due Wed Wk 5 (Sep 16). The Module 2 listening response is due Mon Wk 5 (Sep 14), and the Project 1 peer response is due Mon Wk 6 (Sep 21).

### Module 3: recording, sample prep & library building · Wks 6–9

Students record their own sound in the lab and on their phones, prepare it in Audacity, and learn the mixer (the Toft ATB) through a live-sound scenario and a studio-recording walkthrough of MB2508. The deliverable is the midterm on Wed Wk 9 (Oct 14): Project 2, a sample library of at least 20 prepped sounds (20 to 25 as the target), due at the start of class, then the terminology exam in the same session. Library files are mono WAV, 48 kHz, 24-bit, named `[category]-[descriptor]-[variant].wav`. The Module 3 listening response is due Mon Wk 9 (Oct 12), and the midterm peer response is due Mon Wk 10 (Oct 19).

### Module 4: the DAW · Wks 10–13

Three weeks in Ableton Live 11 (audio editing and warping, sampling with Simpler and Drum Rack from a MIDI keyboard, mixing), working from each student's own Module 3 library, then one session in Adobe Audition (Lab 4, Mon Wk 13). The Module 4 listening response is due Mon Wk 13 (Nov 9). The deliverable is the final project: Draft 1 due Wed Wk 14 (Nov 18), Draft 2 due Wed Wk 15 (Dec 2), and the final version due during finals week, exported as `lastname-final.wav` (48 kHz, 32-bit). The cumulative final exam is a separate assessment during finals week.

## conventions

Build conventions (naming, document chrome, dates, the visual system) are in [`under-the-hood/meta/build-conventions.md`](under-the-hood/meta/build-conventions.md), with page templates beside it and per-asset specs in [`assets/asset-recipes.md`](assets/asset-recipes.md). Anyone drafting new student-facing material reads them first. Teaching from the finished pages doesn't require them.

Student-facing pages are HTML sharing `assets/style.css`. TA notes, specs, and policies are Markdown.

The file workflow is local-first, with the class server as sync. Students work in `~/Documents/[netid]/` on whichever lab computer they're at, download from the server at the start of a session, and upload at the end, using FileZilla. The server is reachable from the lab only.

## for the TA

To prep a week, open the module's `README.md`: it has the session-by-session plan (what to say, where students get stuck, how to use each tool, what to assess and how).

Faculty reference texts for the materials (not assigned to students): Hosken, *An Introduction to Music Technology* (primary); Huber, *Modern Recording Techniques*; Holmes, *Electronic and Experimental Music*; *The Cambridge Companion to Electronic Music*.
