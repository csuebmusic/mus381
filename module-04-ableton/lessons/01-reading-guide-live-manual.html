Module 04 · Audio, sampling & mixing in Ableton

Reading the Live Manual

Where to read in Ableton's own manual, week by week, and the words to keep from each part.

Ableton Live comes with a full reference manual, and learning to find your way around it is a real skill: every DAW has one, and the answer to most questions is in it. This guide sends you to the parts that matter for this module, in the order we reach them. We read the linked sections together in class. Under each link are the vocabulary words from that section, the ones worth remembering.

How to use this

Open a link (each opens in a new tab), skim the section before class, and bring questions. In class we walk it together. After, the vocabulary list under each link is your quick reference: if you can say what each word means, you have what you need from that section.

Week 10 · The environment and audio editing

§ First Steps Setup and the main window · opens in a new tab ↗
Control Bar
The strip across the top of the window: the play and stop buttons, the tempo, and the switch between Live's two views.
Browser
The panel down the left side where your sounds, instruments, and effects live.
Info View
The box at the bottom left that names whatever you point your mouse at. Your fastest way to learn what a control does.
§ Live Concepts How Live is put together · opens in a new tab ↗
Live Set
The document you work in, the file that holds your piece.
Project folder
The folder that keeps a Set and the sounds it uses together in one place.
Arrangement View
The timeline view: time runs left to right. Home base this module.
Session View
The grid view: slots you start and stop on their own.
Track
A lane that holds clips and plays one at a time. Both views share the same tracks.
Audio track and MIDI track
An audio track carries recorded sound; a MIDI track carries note data. This module starts with audio.
Clip
A single piece of sound on a track. An audio clip is a reference to a sample, not the sound itself.
Sample
The sound file a clip points at. Your Module 3 library is made of samples.
Nondestructive editing
Edits live on the clip and shape playback; the sample on disk is never changed.
§ Arrangement View Audio clips on the timeline · opens in a new tab ↗
Clip start and end
Where in the file a clip begins and ends. Drag the edge to change them.
Fade and crossfade
A volume ramp at a clip's edge; a crossfade is the overlap between two clips.
Split
Cut one clip into two.
Consolidate
Merge a selection of clips into a single clip.
§ Clip View A clip's own settings · opens in a new tab ↗
Clip View
The panel along the bottom, opened by double-clicking a clip, where its settings live.
Clip gain
A clip's own volume, set without touching the file.
Transpose (pitch)
Shift a clip up or down in semitones.
Loop
Set a clip to repeat a chosen region.
§ Audio Clips, Tempo, and Warping Matching sound to tempo · opens in a new tab ↗
Tempo (BPM)
The project's speed, set in the Control Bar.
Warp
Stretching a sample's speed to match the tempo, without changing its pitch.
Warp markers
Points that pin a moment in the sample to a moment in the bar.
Warp on and off
On for loops you want locked to the grid; off for one-shots at their recorded speed.

Week 11 · Sampling and MIDI as a trigger

This week your library stops being a folder and becomes an instrument you play.

§ Simpler Play one sample across the keyboard · opens in a new tab ↗
Instrument
A device that turns MIDI notes into sound.
Simpler
An instrument that plays one sample across the keyboard, higher or lower with the note.
Volume envelope (attack, decay, sustain, release)
How a single note's loudness rises and falls.
§ Drum Racks A kit of one-sample pads · opens in a new tab ↗
Drum Rack
A grid of pads, each pad holding one sample.
Pad
One slot in the rack, triggered by one MIDI note.
§ Editing MIDI Notes and Velocities Drawing the notes that trigger an instrument · opens in a new tab ↗
MIDI note
Data, not sound: which pitch, when, and how hard. It triggers an instrument.
Velocity
How hard a note is played.
Piano roll
The grid where you draw and edit MIDI notes.
MIDI clip
A clip made of MIDI notes rather than audio.

Week 12 · Mixing

The same moves you met on the Toft console in Module 3, now in software you drive yourself.

§ Monitoring When you hear a track's input · opens in a new tab ↗
Monitor
The setting that decides whether you hear the signal coming into a track.
§ Internal Routings Where a track's signal comes from and goes · opens in a new tab ↗
Routing
Choosing a track's input source and output destination.
§ Mixing The Live mixer as the digital console · opens in a new tab ↗
Track fader
A track's volume. The channel fader from the Module 3 console.
Pan
A track's left-to-right placement.
Send and return track
A send routes a copy of a track to a return that holds a shared effect. The aux send from the Toft.
Group track
Several tracks folded into one for combined level and processing. A subgroup.
Master track
The final bus every track passes through on the way out.
§ Live Audio Effect Reference The module's effects, as Live devices · opens in a new tab ↗
Insert effect
An effect in a track's own chain, where the order matters: Utility, EQ Eight, Auto Filter, Compressor, then bus and master tools (Glue Compressor, Limiter, Multiband Dynamics).
Send effect
An effect on a return track, shared across tracks: Hybrid Reverb, Reverb, Delay, Echo.
EQ Eight
Live's equalizer. The EQ from Module 2, as a Live device.
Compressor and Glue Compressor
Dynamics control on a single track, or across a bus or the master.

Week 13 · The same ideas in another DAW

This week steps outside Ableton into Adobe Audition, so there is no Live-manual reading. The point is that the skills are portable: the words below name the ideas you carry from one DAW to the next.

Transferable concepts
Sample rate, bit depth, editing, fades, EQ, compression, multitrack. Every serious audio tool has them, under its own names.

Across the module · Exporting

§ Exporting Audio and Video Turning a Set into a finished file · opens in a new tab ↗
Export (render)
Writing your Set out to a single audio file.
48 kHz / 32-bit
The format you export at in this module: the same rate you have used all along, with bit depth stepped up at the end.
Collect All and Save
Gathering a Set's samples into its Project folder so the whole piece travels as one.
