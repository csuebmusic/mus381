Course description

An introduction to the fundamental concepts of music technology, including acoustics and the elements of sound, audio recording, sample editing, and overall sound mixing. The course also briefly surveys advanced topics in the field, such as DSP and synthesis.

The work is hands-on and project-based. The semester runs in four modules, and each one ends in something you make: a short composition, a sample library, a finished produced piece. You will spend most of your time listening closely and building, not memorizing. No musical-technology background is needed to start; we build the vocabulary together.

A note on listening safely

This course asks you to listen closely, often on headphones and on studio monitors, for long stretches. Damage from loud sound builds up gradually and does not heal.

A few practices we use all semester:

  • Keep monitoring at a moderate level. You hear detail better there anyway.
  • Take breaks. Step away from the headphones every hour or so and give your ears some quiet.
  • Be extra careful with headphones and in the studio (MB2508), where the level can climb without your noticing.
  • If your ears ring or sound feels muffled after a session, treat it as a warning. Lower the level and rest your ears before going on.
One rule to remember

If you have to raise your voice to talk over what you are monitoring, it is too loud. Turn it down.

Course structure

The semester runs in four modules. Mondays are lecture: a concept and a listening example. Wednesdays are lab in MB2525, where you work at a station and build the week's skill hands-on.

  • Module 1, Computer and studio fluency (Wk 1). Finding your way around the lab Macs, the file workflow, and the gear.
  • Module 2, Digital audio, editing, and mixing (Wks 2 to 5). Editing and arranging sound in Audacity, ending in Project 1, a short musique concrète piece built from provided sounds.
  • Module 3, Recording and sample library (Wks 6 to 9). Recording your own sound and preparing it for use, ending in the midterm: a sample library you build, plus a terminology exam.
  • Module 4, The DAW (Wks 10 to 13). Producing in Ableton Live from your own library, ending in the final project, drafted in Wks 14 and 15 and completed during finals.

All course materials live on the course website at csuebmusic.github.io/mus381, organized by module, and on Canvas.

Student learning outcomes

Upon successful completion of this course, you will be able to:

  • Discuss and evaluate the place of music technology in the music industry.
  • Identify and discuss the fundamental concepts of acoustics and digital audio.
  • Identify and explain the basic elements of MIDI.
  • Use basic recording and sound reinforcement equipment for musical projects.
  • Use music software for digital audio, synthesis, sequencing, and music notation.

Required materials

There is no required textbook. All readings, handouts, and interactive tools live on the course website and on Canvas, free to you.

The lab provides everything you need to work in person: Mac workstations with Audacity and Ableton Live installed, audio interfaces, microphones, MIDI keyboards, and headphones. Beyond that, you will need:

  • A way to store and move your work. The class server holds the master copy: you download your work at the start of each session and upload it at the end, moving files with an app called FileZilla. You can reach the server from inside the lab only. Day 1 sets this up with you.
  • A smartphone for field recording in Module 3. Any recent phone works; we install a free recording app together.
  • A Canvas account, where written work is submitted.

You are welcome to bring your own wired headphones if you prefer them to the lab pairs.

Assignments and deliverables

Your grade is built from the work you make across the four modules, plus your listening responses and the in-class labs.

  • Project 1 (Module 2): a short musique concrète piece built from provided sounds, showing your editing and mixing. Due Wk 5.
  • Midterm (Module 3): a sample library you record, prepare, and organize, plus an in-class terminology exam covering Modules 1 to 3. Due Wk 9.
  • Final project (Module 4): a piece built in Ableton from your own recorded sound, drafted twice (Wks 14 and 15) and completed during finals.
  • Final exam: a cumulative in-class exam during finals week.
  • Listening responses: short written responses to the historical listening in each module, plus peer responses to classmates' work in Modules 2 and 3.
  • Weekly labs: the Wednesday hands-on tutorials, where the skills are built.

Written work (listening responses, peer responses, the final exam) is submitted on Canvas. Audio deliverables go to the class server, following the workflow you learn on Day 1.

Use of generative AI

Generative AI tools (ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, and the like) are not permitted for any graded work in this course. The written work here is short and personal: your listening responses and your peer reviews are your own listening and your own words.

You may use AI privately as a study aid: to have a concept explained a different way, or to quiz yourself before the terminology exam. What you may not do is use it to write, draft, edit, or revise anything you hand in. If you are ever unsure whether a use is allowed, ask me first.

Time commitment and attendance

This is a 3-unit, in-person course. Plan on the three hours a week we meet plus roughly six hours a week outside class for listening, reading, lab work, and projects. The Wednesday labs are where the hands-on skills are built, and they are hard to make up alone, so steady attendance matters more here than in a lecture course. If you have to miss a session, email me ahead of time and we will sort out how to keep you on track.

Late work

I accept late work. Assignments submitted after the deadline lose 2% per day late, up to a maximum deduction of 50%. Submitting late is always better than not submitting: lateness alone never drops work below 50%, and the quality of the work is still evaluated normally. If you anticipate a real problem meeting a deadline, reach out early and we will work something out.

Accessibility and student support

If you need accommodations, please contact Accessibility Services as early in the term as you can, and let me know how I can help. Because this course leans heavily on listening, reach out early if you anticipate needing any audio accommodations, and we will plan ahead together. Additional support, including tutoring, advising, library services, and technical help, is available through MyCompass.
