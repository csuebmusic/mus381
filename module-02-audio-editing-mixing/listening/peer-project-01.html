peer listening: Project 1

Listen to your classmates' Project 1 pieces and respond to a few that caught your attention.

After Wednesday's submission, the class listening folder fills up with everyone's Project 1 pieces. Listen to all of them carefully, notice the choices your classmates made in their first pieces, and write briefly about three or four that caught your attention. We'll discuss the pieces briefly at the start of class on Mon Wk 6. Come prepared.

how to listen

The class listening folder is at /public/mus-381-fall-2026/project-01-pieces/ on the class server. Connect with FileZilla, click the / at the top of the directory tree on the right side, then open public and navigate down to the folder. You'll see ~25 files, one per classmate.

You can reach the server from inside the lab only. Download the whole folder there, and put a copy on a USB drive or your own cloud storage if you want to listen somewhere else.

Listen on headphones if possible. Listen all the way through each piece, even when it doesn't hold your interest right away. Most of these pieces are 90 seconds to 2 minutes long, and the full folder is roughly 45 minutes of listening. Set aside a quiet block of time for it.

Don't multitask while listening.

what to write

Pick any three or four pieces that caught your attention, for whatever reason. They might be pieces that surprised you, that did something you wouldn't have thought of, or that you wish you'd made.

For each piece you write about:

  1. Say who made it (the filename or last name).
  2. Name one specific thing you noticed, such as a sound choice, a structural decision, a transition, a moment of contrast, a use of silence, or a particular edit. For example: "the sudden cut at around 0:40 changed the whole feel of the piece."
  3. Ask the maker one question you're curious about, such as "How did you decide to put the metallic sounds at the end instead of the beginning?" or "Was the long silence in the middle intentional, and how did you choose its length?" Ask real questions, not rhetorical ones.

Write about 50 to 80 words per piece, and keep each one specific.

tone

Be generous and curious. If a piece didn't work for you, write about something else (there's no requirement to critique). The pieces you choose to write about are a form of feedback too.

The questions you ask will sometimes get answered in the Mon Wk 6 discussion. Bring them.

Submission details
Where
Upload to Canvas under this assignment.
File format
Word document (.docx) or PDF (.pdf).
Filename
lastname-peer-listening-02.docx (lowercase, hyphens, your last name).
Length
About 200 to 320 words total: 50 to 80 words for each of 3 or 4 pieces. Concise is fine; don't pad.
Formatting
12 pt, double-spaced, 1-inch margins. Use Times New Roman, Garamond, or another standard serif. Include your name, the course (MUS 381), the assignment, and the date at the top.
Citations
Not required. Identify each piece by your classmate's last name or filename.
Due
Mon Wk 6, before class.
A note on giving and receiving feedback

What you write about a classmate's piece may end up in front of them in some form. Write it the way you'd want to read feedback on your own piece.
