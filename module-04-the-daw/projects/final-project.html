Final project

A finished piece, 2 to 3 minutes long, built in Ableton from sound you recorded yourself.

This is the last thing you make in the course, and it should show what you can now do: record sound, edit it, sample it, arrange it, and mix it into a finished piece. Build it in Ableton Live, 2 to 3 minutes long, entirely from sound you recorded yourself. The approach is open. You can arrange edited audio clips the way you did in Project 1, build the piece from samples you trigger and chop with Simpler and the Drum Rack, or combine both. There is no required style. What the piece has to do is sound like you meant every choice in it.

Constraints

  • Length: 2 to 3 minutes.
  • Source material: every sound in the piece must be one you recorded yourself. Your Module 3 sample library counts, and so does anything new you record between now and the deadline. No pre-recorded, found, or downloaded sound, and no pre-made loops. (One exception, with its own rule: see Recording and sourcing below.)
  • Software: Ableton Live.
  • Format: final export as WAV, 48 kHz, 32-bit float (the Module 4 standard).
  • Filename: lastname-final.wav (see Submission below for the full file flow).

What "demonstrating fluency" means

A successful piece will show that you can:

  • Record and choose sound with intention: your material is yours, captured and picked deliberately rather than grabbed at random.
  • Edit deliberately: cuts, fades, and placements are decisions, not accidents.
  • Sample: load a sound into Simpler or the Drum Rack, chop it, and play it. Use this where it serves the piece; you don't have to.
  • Arrange: the piece is built across the Arrangement timeline with a deliberate shape.
  • Mix: levels are balanced, each element has space, nothing clips.

You don't need to use every skill equally. A piece that does three things well beats one that crams in everything Ableton offers.

Form, again

You met form in Project 1: the overall shape of a piece across its full length, the thing that turns a sequence of sounds into a piece of music. The same applies here, with more room. Two to three minutes needs a deliberate shape, whether that's sections that contrast and return, a single arc from sparse to dense, or a steady groove that earns its length. Before you call it done, you should be able to draw your piece on a napkin. If you want the longer treatment, the form section of Project 1 still holds.

Recording and sourcing your sound

The rule is simple: you recorded everything you hear. That is the point of the project, to make something from sound you went out and captured, the way the artists in this module's listening built their work from records they dug up. Your Module 3 library is the obvious starting point. Beyond it, record more: the studio, your phone at 48 kHz, an instrument, a hallway, water, a voice.

One exception, and it comes with a rule of its own. You may use a sound from another student's library, but only if you ask them first and they agree, and only if you credit them. This is how sourcing works outside the classroom. You do not take someone's recording without permission, and when you use it, you say whose it is. Keep a short credits file, lastname-final-credits.txt, in your working folder, listing any borrowed sound and the person it came from. If everything is your own, the file can simply say so.

Process and schedule

This is the largest thing you make all term, so the schedule is built around drafts:

  • Draft 1, due Wed Wk 14: a complete rough pass. The whole piece exists end to end, even if it is unmixed and rough. You submit it to your working folder, and the instructor and TA give you written feedback to revise on.
  • Draft 2, due Wed Wk 15: the revision. You have taken the Draft 1 feedback and acted on it.
  • Final, during finals week: polished and mixed, exported and submitted (see Submission below).
  • Start in the Wk 12 to 13 labs, not in Wk 14.
  • Save versions: lastname-final-v1, v2, v3. You'll want to be able to go back.
  • Use lab time for feedback. The TA and your classmates are resources while you build.

Rubric

The final project is graded out of 100 points across four dimensions.

Technique & tools (35)

  • The semester's skills are visible and used well: editing, sampling, arrangement, mixing
  • Where you sample (Simpler, Drum Rack), it is done with control, not by accident
  • The DAW is used deliberately; the piece shows real work, not an afternoon's throw-together

Form & shape (30)

  • The piece has a deliberate shape across its 2 to 3 minutes: beginning, middle, end, in some form
  • Pacing is intentional; the length feels earned, not padded or rushed
  • It goes somewhere; it isn't one texture for three minutes

Sound material & sourcing (20)

  • Every sound is your own recording, or borrowed with permission and properly credited
  • Material is chosen and recorded with intention, and prepared cleanly
  • A credits file is present, even if it just says everything is yours

Mix & craft (15)

  • Levels are balanced; nothing is buried or overpowering
  • Nothing clips; the export is clean
  • Elements have space; the stereo field is considered

What I'm not grading

  • Style or genre. A beat-driven track and an abstract sound-collage can both score 100.
  • Technical perfection. Intention and craft matter more than polish.
  • Length within the range. A 2:10 piece and a 2:55 piece are both fine. Don't pad to hit a number.
  • Whether I personally like the piece. The rubric is the rubric.
