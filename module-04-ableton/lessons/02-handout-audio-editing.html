Audio Editing in Ableton

Bring your library into Live and edit it on the timeline.

Monday you read about clips, nondestructive editing, and what a DAW is. Today you put that to work: pull your sample library into Live and edit it on the Arrangement timeline. Each step below says what to do and links the manual page that shows how. By the end you will have a short arrangement built from your own sounds, saved and uploaded.

Today's gear

Take from the lab's gear storage: audio interface, headphones. Plug in and run through the start-of-session steps on the Session Routines card before continuing here.

What we're doing today

  1. Pull your sample library down from the NAS to your working folder.
  2. Open a new Live Set and import several sounds from your library.
  3. Place them as clips on the Arrangement timeline.
  4. Edit the clips: trim start and end, set clip gain, add fades.
  5. Try warping on a clip: on for a loop, off for a one-shot.
  6. Arrange three or more sounds into a short piece, about 30 seconds.
  7. Collect All and Save, export, and upload to the NAS.
Before you start

This is the first time you open Live and the first time you pull your own library into a project. The mechanics live in Ableton's manual and the Learn Live videos linked from Monday's reading. When a step links a manual page, open it, do the move, come back. Keep the Session Routines card visible.

Get your library and start a Set

During your start-of-session routine, while the NAS is still mounted, copy your sample library from [path]/students/lastname/sample-library/ down into your local working folder at ~/Documents/lastname/. Work from the local copy, never from the NAS.

Open Live and start a new Set. Save it right away into your working folder and name it lastname-wk10. Saving now creates the Project folder that will hold this session's work.

Bring in your sounds

Pick four to six sounds from your library that you might want to use. Preview them in Live's browser, then drag each one onto its own audio track in the Arrangement view. Dragging from a Finder window works too.

Aim for variety: a sustained sound or two, a short hit or two, and at least one rhythmic loop if your library has one. You will want the contrast when you arrange.

Edit the clips

Work on each clip in turn and practice the four basic moves. Trim the start and the end so the clip holds just the part you want. Set the clip's gain so it sits at a comfortable level. Add a short fade in and a short fade out so nothing starts or ends with a click.

The moves all live in Clip View. The manual page and its Learn Live video show each one:

Clip View (manual)

Edit freely. Nothing you do here changes the files in your library; you are editing clips, not the sounds underneath.

Warp one clip

Warping is how Live stretches a clip to match the Set's tempo. Try it both ways on two different sounds.

Take a rhythmic loop and turn Warp on. The loop now locks to the Set tempo, so it stays in time if you change the tempo. Take a one-shot, a single hit or a short sound with no built-in rhythm, and turn Warp off. It plays at its recorded speed. The rule of thumb: loops usually want Warp on, one-shots usually want it off.

Warp has several modes (Beats, Tones, Texture, Complex). Leave them at the default for today. The how, and what each mode is for, is here:

Audio Clips, Tempo, and Warping (manual)

Build a short arrangement

Arrange three or more of your edited sounds into about thirty seconds of music. Duplicate clips, move them along the timeline, and layer them across tracks. There are no rules about what it should sound like. The point is the moves: placing, trimming, fading, and stacking clips until you have something you chose the shape of.

Keep this short piece. It is practice, and it is also raw material you can pull back into a later session.

Export

Export the arrangement to a single audio file at 48 kHz, 32-bit, saved into your working folder. Name it lastname-wk10-edit.wav.

Exporting Audio and Video (manual)

End of session

What to upload today

  1. In Live, run File → Collect All and Save. This copies the library sounds you used into the Set's Project folder, so the project travels as one piece and nothing goes missing.
  2. During the NAS-upload phase of the routine, upload your lastname/ working folder to [path]/students/lastname/, choosing Replace when prompted. The lastname-wk10 Project folder and your exported WAV go up with it.
  3. Verify the Project folder and lastname-wk10-edit.wav are on the NAS.

Then continue with the rest of the card's end-of-session routine: eject the NAS, sign out of browser accounts, quit all apps, knobs back to zero, unplug everything, stow the gear back in the lab's gear storage, chair in.
