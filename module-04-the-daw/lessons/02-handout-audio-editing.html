Audio Editing in Ableton

Set up Live, bring your library onto the timeline, and edit it into a short piece.

Monday you read about clips, nondestructive editing, and what a DAW is. Today you open Live for the first time and put that reading to work: set up the program, pull your sample library onto the Arrangement timeline, and edit it into a short piece. By the end you will have a thirty second arrangement built from your own sounds, saved in two versions and uploaded.

Today's gear

Take from the lab's gear storage: audio interface, headphones. Plug in and run through the start-of-session steps on the Session Routines card before continuing here.

What we're doing today

  1. Set up Live: the audio interface, the ins and outs, and your first save.
  2. Bring your sample library into Live and onto the Arrangement timeline.
  3. Edit the clips: trim, set clip gain, add fades.
  4. Arrange three or more sounds into about thirty seconds.
  5. Save a second version and try warping in it.
  6. Export, Collect All and Save, and upload to the NAS.

If you hear nothing

Sound travels from Live to the interface to your headphones. If a clip plays and you hear nothing, check three things: Live's audio device is set to your interface, your headphones are in the interface and not the Mac, and the interface headphone level is up. Turn that level low before your first play and bring it up from there, so a loud sample does not catch you off guard.

Edit the clips

Work on each clip in turn. Trim the start and the end so the clip holds just the part you want, set the clip's gain so it sits at a comfortable level, and add a short fade in and fade out so nothing starts or ends with a click.

Most clip edits live in the Detail View, the panel along the bottom of the screen. Open it and you see the selected clip up close in Clip View.

▶ The Detail View and Clip View Video ↗ § Clip View Live 11 manual ↗

Two controls in Clip View are worth a close look.

Notice the clip's pitch control. Changing the pitch also changes the clip's length, the way speeding up or slowing down tape does. That is specific to repitching a clip this way. When you play sounds from a sampling instrument in Wk 11, you repitch them a different way. Set it back to zero when you are done looking, so it does not shift the sound in your arrangement.

Now the gain slider. Drag it and watch the waveform in the clip grow and shrink. That gain belongs to the clip, and it is the first of several places you set level in Live.

Gain staging

Clip gain is one of several places level gets set on the way to the speakers, and it is the one you meet first. It belongs to the clip, not the track: every clip carries its own. Setting it is a little like normalizing a file, except the file never changes. The diagram shows the stages a sound passes through, from the clip gain you are using now out to the master.

Edit freely. Nothing you do here changes the files in your library; you are editing clips, not the sounds underneath.

Build a short arrangement

Arrange three or more of your edited sounds into about thirty seconds of music. Duplicate clips, move them along the timeline, and layer them across tracks. There are no rules about what it should sound like. The point is the moves: placing, trimming, fading, and stacking clips until you have something whose shape you chose. Save as you go with Cmd-S.

Save a version, then warp

Your arrangement is saved as lastname-wk10-v1. Before you experiment with warping, branch it: run File → Save As and name the copy lastname-wk10-v2. Do all your warping in v2, so v1 keeps the arrangement exactly as you built it. Saving a fresh version before you experiment is a habit worth keeping, since it lets you try anything and still walk back.

Warping is how Live stretches a clip to match the Set's tempo. Try it both ways on two different sounds. Take a rhythmic loop and turn Warp on: the loop locks to the Set tempo and stays in time if you change the tempo. Take a one-shot, a single hit with no built-in rhythm, and turn Warp off: it plays at its recorded speed. Loops usually want Warp on, one-shots usually want it off.

End of session

What to upload today

  1. In Live, run File → Collect All and Save on your v2 Set. This copies the library sounds you used into the Set's Project folder, so the project travels as one piece and nothing goes missing. Do the same for v1 if it is still open.
  2. During the NAS-upload phase of the routine, upload your lastname/ working folder to [path]/students/lastname/, choosing Replace when prompted. Both Project folders (v1 and v2) and your exported WAV go up with it.
  3. Verify the lastname-wk10-v1 and lastname-wk10-v2 Project folders and lastname-wk10-edit.wav are on the NAS.

Then continue with the rest of the card's end-of-session routine: eject the NAS, sign out of browser accounts, quit all apps, knobs back to zero, unplug everything, stow the gear back in the lab's gear storage, chair in.
