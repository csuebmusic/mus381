Project 1: musique concrète

A short piece built from a curated sample bank, using the editing vocabulary from Module 2.

Build a short piece, up to 2 minutes long, entirely from source sounds provided on the class server. The piece should demonstrate the editing and mixing skills you've learned in Module 2. This is your first piece in the course: make something with intention, where every cut, fade, and mix decision is yours. There is no stylistic requirement beyond "use the source sounds creatively": your piece can be rhythmic, abstract, narrative, atmospheric, or anything else.

constraints

  • The piece is 90 seconds to 2 minutes long.
  • Use only sounds from the project sample bank at /public/sample-banks/project-01/. You may use any sound from the bank, in any combination, processed in any way.
  • Work in Audacity.
  • Export the final piece as a WAV, 48 kHz, 24-bit.
  • Name the file lastname-project01.wav (see Submission below for where each file goes).

what "demonstrating skills" means

A successful piece shows five things.

  • You listened first and chose source sounds with intention.
  • You edited deliberately, and every cut, fade, and placement in time is a choice you made.
  • You built structure: the piece changes over time instead of holding one texture for its whole length (see the section on structure below).
  • You mixed with care. Levels are balanced, EQ keeps sounds in the same frequency range from covering each other up (masking), and stereo placement is considered.
  • You used the techniques covered in Module 2, and they're audible in the piece.

You don't need to use every technique. Three editing techniques used with control can make a complete piece.

on structure

For most of you this will be the first time you've thought about form, the overall shape of a piece across its full duration. Form is one of the things that turns a sequence of sounds into a piece of music, and you decide it.

One way in is to think of your piece as a small set of sections, each with its own character. A section might be defined by a particular sound, texture, density, or emotional quality. Sections let the listener hear where one idea ends and the next begins.

One classical shape, useful as a starting point, is ABA:

Imagine your sounds fall into two groups. Section A might be built from sharp metallic hits arranged sparsely. Section B might be built from sustained water textures, dense and continuous. The return to A might bring back the metallic hits, layered with fragments of the water texture, and the listener hears both groups together. That's a complete piece in three sections.

ABA is one possibility. Other shapes you might try:

  • A through-composed piece has no return. It moves through a sequence of ideas and ends somewhere new.
  • A narrative arc moves in a single direction across the whole piece: quiet to loud, sparse to dense, chaotic to calm, or the reverse. The change is gradual rather than sectional.
  • A terraced shape has sections that progressively add or remove elements, each new section adding more, or stripping more away, than the last.
  • A drone with events has a continuous underlying texture (a single sustained sound, or a slowly shifting one) with discrete events placed over it. The drone holds steady while the events bring change.

Before you finish, you should be able to sketch your piece on paper: how many sections it has, what changes between them, and where you want the listener's attention.

process expectations

Working habits for this project:

  • Start early. First experiments happen in the Wed Wk 3 lab; don't wait until Wk 5.
  • Save versions of your Audacity project as you go: lastname-project01-v1.aup3, v2, v3. The WAV you hand in is the export of your final version.
  • Take breaks, and come back to the mix with fresh ears. Mixing decisions tend to get worse the longer you work without a rest.
  • Ask your instructor and your classmates for feedback during lab time.

rubric

Project 1 is graded out of 100 points across five dimensions, equally weighted at 20 points each.

source material use (20)

  • Sounds chosen with intention, not at random
  • Variety used effectively (or limitation used effectively, if intentional)
  • Source material is recognizable as such, transformed creatively, or somewhere in between (your call, but defensible)

editing craft (20)

  • Cuts are clean: no clicks, pops, or accidental cutoffs
  • Fades used where needed: no abrupt unintended starts/stops
  • Selections are precise: silence between events is intentional
  • Audacity techniques from Module 2 are audible in the piece

mixing craft (20)

  • Levels are balanced: every element is audible at the level you intended
  • EQ keeps sounds in the same frequency range from masking each other
  • Stereo placement is considered
  • Compression and limiting (if used) keep the loud and quiet contrasts the piece needs

structure & form (20)

  • The piece has shape: beginning, middle, end (in some form)
  • Time is used intentionally: pacing decisions are audible
  • The length fits the material, without padding or rushing

process & craft (20)

  • Project folder is organized: versions saved, files named per convention
  • Final export is correctly formatted (WAV, 48 kHz, 24-bit, named correctly)
  • Submitted on time, in both the private working folder and the class listening folder
  • Evidence of revision: the final piece is clearly not a first draft

what isn't graded

  • Style or genre isn't graded: a noisy abstract piece and a rhythmic structured piece can both score 100.
  • Technical perfection isn't expected. Module 2 is the first time most of you have done any of this, and the rubric looks for effort and attention.
  • Where your length falls within the 90-second to 2-minute range doesn't affect the grade. A 95-second piece and a 2-minute piece are both fine; don't pad to hit a number.
  • Your instructor grades by the rubric, and personal taste isn't part of it.
